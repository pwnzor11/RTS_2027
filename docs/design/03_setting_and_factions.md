# Design 03 · Setting and factions
From D-020, D-027, D-028, D-029, D-032. Everything below the "Decided" line is a proposal for the
owner to shape; nothing here is final until it is in DECISIONS.

## Decided
- **Premise:** October 1962 went nuclear. The game is set around 2001 on a scarred Earth where the
  transistor age never came: hardened vacuum tubes, nuclear-powered vehicles, 1960s industrial design
  pushed to extremes. Atompunk, played straight.
- **Resource:** **ashfall**, radioactive ash that still falls from the dust belt and the burning
  reactor cities, drifting into fields that are scraped up and refined into fissile concentrate.
  **Pitchblende** pits are the rare rich tier. Ashfall harms unprotected infantry.
- **Factions:** the **Continental Reconstruction Authority** (protagonist) and the **Directorate of
  the Interior** (antagonist, the more fun side). Campaigns are parallel and opposed.
- **Theaters:** **Rust Belt** (ruined industrial cities under snow and black ash) and **Glass Coast**
  (fused desert, drowned suburbs, grey ash dunes).

## The world (proposal)
Forty years after the exchange, two powers have crawled out of the wreckage of North America (the
exact geography can stay vague). Nobody has the industry to rebuild semiconductors, so everything
that works is big, hot, tube-driven and nuclear. Maps, radio and paper are how wars are run.
Fallout still comes down as ash; it is the only energy source left that scales, and the land under
the richest drifts is the land worth dying for.

## The Authority (protagonist)
- **Identity:** the civil government that kept functioning: engineers, convoys, surveyors, a citizen
  army. Orderly, grey-green, stencilled, reluctant. Its briefings are teletype printouts.
- **How it plays:** conventional combined arms done well. Fixed bases with proper walls and power
  grids, heavy tracked armour, artillery, a real air force (prop and early jet), engineers who repair
  and capture, infantry that depends on vehicles for ashfall protection (sealed APCs, decon units).
  Strengths: durable, strong defence, best air, best late-game artillery. Weaknesses: slow, expensive,
  needs power and roads.
- **Harvesting:** armoured scraper → fixed centrifuge refinery. Silos are concrete bunkers.
- **Superweapon ideas:** (1) *Atomic artillery battery*, a railway-gun shell with a long charge;
  (2) *Recon satellite pass / high-altitude strike* from the last bombers, a map-wide reveal plus a
  bombing run. Both need a dedicated structure and a deep chain.
- **Hero/anchor characters:** a field commander who speaks to the player (the "EVA"), a civilian
  administrator who sets objectives, a defector from the Directorate mid-campaign.

## The Directorate (antagonist)
- **Identity:** the continuity-of-government bunker that never came up. Decades underground, it has
  become a theatrical, paranoid state run by a Director nobody has seen since the sixties, speaking
  through loudspeakers and radio. Red and bone-white, brutalist, Cyrillic-free (not the Soviets; the
  menace is domestic). Its briefings are propaganda broadcasts.
- **How it plays:** asymmetric and unfair-feeling by design. Most of its base is **underground**:
  structures are entrances and vents on the surface with hitpoints, while the real machinery is
  implied below. **Tunnelers** (burrow locomotion) move under the map and surface anywhere not built
  on rock; a tunnel network lets units move between its own entrances instantly. Mass **conscripts**
  that are cheap, fast to produce and expendable (ashfall barely bothers them because they are
  already dying). **Radiation weapons** that poison ground, denying it to Authority infantry and
  slowly killing everything that stays. Fewer, stranger vehicles: reactor-cored crawlers, tracked
  loudspeaker units that boost nearby conscripts, a rail-mounted gun that is also its mobile HQ.
  No conventional air; instead balloons/dirigibles and rocket pods. Strengths: mobility through the
  map, swarm, area denial, surprise. Weaknesses: fragile surface structures, poor long-range
  firepower, needs to keep spreading to win.
- **Harvesting:** conscript gangs with carts and a "mobile smelter" that deploys on a field, or
  tunnel-borne scrapers that surface at the drift. Choose one in the unit-roster pass; the point is
  that its economy is faster to set up and easier to kill.
- **Superweapon ideas:** (1) *The Sunrise Device*, a buried warhead detonated under the target
  (long charge, needs a tunnel head near the target); (2) *Broadcast*, a propaganda surge that
  temporarily turns enemy infantry in a radius. Both need a dedicated structure and a deep chain.
- **Anchor characters:** the Director's Voice (the "EVA", unctuous and menacing), a Commissar who
  gives orders and may be the true power, an engineer who starts asking questions.

## Asymmetry at a glance
| | Authority | Directorate |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Fixed, walled, surface | Entrances/vents; tunnel network between them |
| Movement | Roads, ramps, air | Burrow under terrain, surface anywhere soft |
| Infantry | Few, protected, needs vehicles | Many, cheap, expendable |
| Vehicles | Heavy, armoured, slow | Few, strange, reactor-cored |
| Air | Real air force | None; dirigibles and rockets |
| Area control | Walls, turrets, artillery | Radiation denial, surprise emergence |
| Economy | Slow to set up, durable | Fast to set up, fragile |
| Win condition feel | Grind forward and hold | Spread, strike, overwhelm |

## Campaign spines (one-line sketches)
- **Authority:** a survey convoy is attacked in the Rust Belt → the Directorate surfaces as a real
  power → the player pushes toward the Glass Coast launch sites the Directorate wants → discovers the
  Director's plan (a second exchange to "finish the war") → final assault on a mountain complex.
  Unlock order follows the army's rebuilding: infantry and scrapers → light vehicles → walls and
  power → armour → artillery → air → upgrades → superweapons in the last third.
- **Directorate:** the Voice orders the player to "reclaim" the surface → expanding tunnel by tunnel
  → sabotaging Authority reactors → the Commissar's purges → the player chooses (choice point) to
  seize the launch sites for the Director or for themselves. Unlock order: conscripts and tunnels →
  radiation → crawlers → the rail gun → Broadcast → the Device.

## Open design threads (for the roster pass, not decisions yet)
Tunnel network rules (capacity, cooldown, visibility to the enemy); how radiation denial interacts
with the Authority's decon units; whether conscripts can be "pressed" from captured civilians; the
exact two superweapons; the naming of individual units (period-correct designations like "M-series"
for the Authority, programme codenames for the Directorate).
